# Desktop

## Signing in

The login screen lists the people of this computer. Your password unlocks
your session, your encrypted home, and your saved passwords. The screen locks
after the idle time you choose in **Settings > Account**, and before the
computer sleeps; the lock screen looks like the login screen.

## Bars

There are two bars, and each can sit on any edge of the screen:

- The **system bar** has the clock and the status items. Each opens a panel:
  **Notifications**, **Microphone** and **Sound**, **Brightness**,
  **Network** (Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, joined and paired right in the panel),
  **Input language**, **Battery** with the power profile, and the **System
  menu** (Settings, Lock, Suspend, Restart, Shut down, Log out).
- The **dock** has **Apps**, a map of each workspace, and the quick actions:
  **Capture**, **Dictation**, and the **Assistant**. The quick actions also
  open from a hot corner.

Apps that keep running without a window show their icons in the middle of the
system bar: a click does what the app does for it, a right click opens its
menu.

In **Settings > Shell**, drag any item to another place, hide it, or make a
dock button half size, so that two share a place.

## Workspaces and windows

The session starts with one workspace. While you drag a window or an app, the
dock shows a **+**: drop it there for a new workspace, up to eight. A
workspace without windows goes away.

Each workspace's map on the dock shows its windows where they are. Drag a
window onto another to swap them, or onto another map to move it there.
Double-click a window on the map for full screen, and right-click it to
close it. The keyboard does all of this too ([Shortcuts](Shortcuts.md)).

Windows tile in the workspace's layout: **Columns** (the default), **Lead**,
**Dwindle**, **Center**, **Grid**, **Rows**, **Tabs**, or **Accordion**. Drag
the handles between windows to resize them.

## Notifications

A new notification shows as a banner near the bell for a few seconds; urgent
ones stay until you dismiss them. Every notification waits in the
**Notifications** panel (the bell, or Super+N), grouped by app, until you
clear it. Notifications are never written to disk and are gone when you log
out.

- **Do Not Disturb** (right-click the bell, or Settings > Notifications)
  holds back banners, but not urgent ones, for an hour, until tomorrow, or
  until you turn it off. Banners are also held back while you share your
  screen.
- **Per app**, Settings > Notifications chooses **Banners**, **Center only**,
  or **Off**.
- The lock screen shows only how many notifications wait.

## Screenshots and recordings

**Capture** (the quick action, or Print) takes screenshots and records the
screen, your voice, or the camera:

- **Screenshots and screen recordings** are of a selection, a window, or the
  whole screen. A screenshot can open for markup first. A screen recording
  can have a timer, the microphone, and the camera in a round bubble you can
  drag anywhere.
- **Voice recordings** go to Music/Recordings, and **camera recordings** to
  Videos/Camera.
- **Skip silence** shortens every quiet stretch of a recording to a short
  pause.

When an app asks to share your screen, the same overlay asks what to share.

## Downloads and extras

Everything the desktop downloads, such as apps, the browser, and extras, waits
in one queue. **Settings > Downloads** shows it: cancel or retry a job there.
Downloads go on if you log out and continue at your next sign-in.

**Extras** are large optional downloads, such as the speech and language
models of [Dictation](Dictation.md) and the [Assistant](Assistant.md). They
are downloaded once for everyone on the computer, and only administrators can
add or remove them, in Welcome or **Settings > Assistant**.

## Settings

Settings (Super+I) has only what works: **Account**, **Assistant**,
**Language**, **Time**, **Desktop**, **Shell**, **Windows**, **Display**,
**Mouse**, **Network**, **Sound**, **Apps**, **Downloads**, **Updates**, and
**Licenses**, with a search across them. Settings for the whole computer,
such as the system language and time zone, ask for an administrator's
password.

## Welcome

At your first sign-in, **Welcome** asks what the installer didn't: the
network, if you're offline; a browser and recommended apps
([Apps](Apps.md)); and, for administrators, the optional extras. It then
shows the main ways to get around. **Skip all** ends it at any step. Open it
again from **Settings > Account**.

Copying and pasting works after the app you copied from closes. Printing and
email aren't available yet.
